abstract - Transformer models learn useful representations for protein classification and generation, but those representations are difficult to interpret. This work adapts attention-analysis methods from NLP to protein language models. Across BERT, ALBERT, and XLNet models trained on two protein datasets, the authors find that attention connects amino acids that are distant in sequence but close in three-dimensional structure, focuses on binding sites, and represents progressively more complex biophysical properties in deeper layers. The study also compares attention with hidden-state representations and presents a visualization of attention in the context of protein structure.
key_findings - Across five pretrained protein Transformer models, some attention heads align strongly with structural contacts, with the most aligned heads directing approximately 44.5%–63.2% of high-confidence attention to contacts, versus a background contact frequency of 1.3%. Attention also concentrates on binding sites, and a small number of heads focus strongly on post-translational modification positions. Attention patterns across heads are correlated with amino-acid substitution relationships represented by BLOSUM matrices. Layerwise analyses indicate that deeper layers tend to emphasize higher-level properties such as contacts and binding sites, while secondary-structure information is distributed more evenly. Hidden-state probes accumulate contact-map information gradually, whereas attention probes capture it mainly in the final layers.
limitation_of_sota - Prior work showed that protein language-model embeddings can encode structural and functional properties, but offered comparatively limited analysis of internal model mechanisms, particularly attention and intermediate representations. Existing NLP interpretability methods also do not directly address protein-specific structural and functional properties, such as three-dimensional contacts, binding sites, and post-translational modifications.
proposed_solution - The paper adapts and extends Transformer interpretability methods to protein sequences. It analyzes pretrained BERT, ALBERT, and XLNet models using protein datasets with structural and functional annotations. For each attention head, it measures the proportion of high-confidence attention arcs aligned with pairwise properties such as three-dimensional contacts, or directed toward token-level properties such as binding sites, secondary-structure classes, post-translational modifications, and particular amino acids. It evaluates whether observed alignments exceed background frequencies using significance tests with Bonferroni correction and compares them with randomized-attention null models. The authors also use linear probing classifiers to compare information in layer output embeddings with information in attention weights, and compare amino-acid attention profiles with BLOSUM substitution scores. Finally, they provide a visualization tool that displays attention in the context of a protein’s three-dimensional structure.
paper_limitations - The analyses are associative: they do not establish that attention causally produces model behavior or that attention explains model predictions. The experiments use selected datasets and annotations, analyze a random subset of 5,000 sequences for attention, truncate sequences to 512 residues, and require available structural or functional labels. The results therefore do not establish that the observed patterns generalize to all proteins or that attention-derived patterns can reliably reveal new biological relationships.
